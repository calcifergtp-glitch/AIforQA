What This Log Is For

This editable log gives a QA team one place to record selected uses of AI tools, what the tool was asked to do, how a person checked the result, and what happened next. It is a starting template. It does not prove that an AI system is validated, that an output is correct, or that a company meets a regulation.

Who May Find It Useful

QA groups, supervisors, and document owners can adapt the log when their organization wants to understand how AI is being used in a defined workflow. First decide whether a log is appropriate for your process. Follow your company’s approved procedures for records, access, retention, and review.

How to Use the Template

  1. Set the rules first. Your organization decides which AI uses may be logged, who reviews the log, where it is stored, and how long it is retained.
  2. Record the use clearly. Note the task, tool and version if available, date, and a short description or approved record reference.
  3. Describe the human check. Say what source or procedure was checked and who performed the review, following your recordkeeping rules.
  4. Record the result. Note whether the output was accepted, edited, rejected, or escalated, and identify any required follow-up.
  5. Review for patterns carefully. A log can support a team discussion about usage and controls. It does not by itself show that AI caused an outcome or that a process is effective.

What to Record—and What to Keep Out

Use only information your organization permits in the log. A short, sanitized description or controlled-record reference is often safer than copying a full prompt or AI response. Do not place patient, employee, batch, formula, deviation, supplier, or other confidential information into a general tracking log unless the system and your procedures explicitly allow it.

Protect the log as a quality record if your intended use or local procedure makes it one. Define access, correction, review, and retention rules before relying on it. Do not treat a spreadsheet or this download as a validated electronic system by default.

Regulatory Context and Limits

For drug CGMP computerized systems, 21 CFR 211.68 addresses appropriate controls over certain equipment and computer-related systems, including checking input and output for accuracy. FDA’s data-integrity guidance discusses reliable and accurate data. ICH Q9(R1) provides quality risk-management guidance.

These sources do not require this particular log or make it a substitute for approved procedures, system controls, required records, or Quality Unit decisions. Assess each AI use according to its actual purpose and impact.
