AI-Assisted Root Cause Analysis in Pharmaceutical Investigations

Compliance
Published 2026-05-31 • Reviewed 2026-11-26 • 3 min read
Root Cause Analysis CAPA Deviations GMP Predictive Analytics Compliance

AI can help investigators search old records, compare similar descriptions, and organize evidence. It cannot establish the root cause of a deviation by itself. A proposed cause is a hypothesis to test, not a conclusion.

In plain words: AI may help find clues. The investigation team checks the evidence, decides what caused the issue, and approves corrective actions.

What an investigation needs to do

For drug products covered by 21 CFR Part 211, section 211.192 requires thorough investigation of unexplained discrepancies and failures to meet specifications, whether or not the batch has already been distributed. FDA’s OOS guidance describes a documented investigation that reviews evidence, evaluates possible causes, and assesses potential impact. ICH Q10 describes a CAPA system tied to investigation, risk, corrective action, and effectiveness review.

The required investigation depth depends on the event and risk. A model-generated explanation is not evidence that the explanation is true.

Where AI may help

  • Search approved deviation, complaint, maintenance, and laboratory records for similar terms.
  • Build a timeline from cited source records for an investigator to verify.
  • Suggest possible factors to examine, such as equipment, method, material, or process changes.
  • Summarize the evidence collected and list gaps that still need an answer.
  • Help track actions and due dates after the investigation team approves them.

AI can miss related records, confuse events that only sound alike, or invent a connection. Keep the source record one click away and make uncertainty visible.

Keep hypotheses separate from findings

Label AI suggestions as unverified. Investigators should test each plausible cause against the records, interviews, process knowledge, and appropriate technical evidence. If evidence does not support a cause, do not keep it just because the model suggested it. If no definitive cause is found, document that uncertainty and follow the site procedure for risk assessment and action.

Do not let an AI tool invalidate an OOS result, choose a retest, assign blame, close a deviation, or approve CAPA. Those actions need an authorized, documented process.

Illustrative example

Illustrative only; this is not a real company or reported result. An investigation team reviews a recurring low-yield event. AI finds two older records with similar wording and links a recent equipment change. The investigator checks the original records, confirms one older event involved a different product, and verifies the change-control record with engineering. The team tests the remaining hypothesis and documents its own conclusion. AI helped locate records; the team established what the evidence supported.

A useful workflow

  1. Define the investigation question and limit the tool to approved records.
  2. Ask for source-linked candidates, not a final root cause.
  3. Verify each record, date, product, equipment, and material against the original.
  4. Record which hypotheses were accepted, rejected, or remain uncertain and why.
  5. Let authorized personnel approve the investigation, CAPA, and effectiveness checks under procedure.
  6. Retain relevant tool output and reviewer decisions when required, and control updates to the workflow.

Key takeaway

AI can speed up searching and help organize evidence. The investigation team must determine what the evidence means, assess product impact, and approve corrective actions. Treat AI output as a lead to check, never as the root cause.
