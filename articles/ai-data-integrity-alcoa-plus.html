AI and Data Integrity: Enforcing ALCOA+ in Pharmaceutical Systems

GxP & Validation
2026-03-10 • 4 min read
Data Integrity ALCOA+ 21 CFR Part 11 Audit Trail Review GxP Computer System Validation

Data integrity means the record can be trusted

In a GMP process, records need to be complete and reliable enough for people to reconstruct what happened and make sound decisions. ALCOA+ is a common way to remember qualities such as attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, accurate, complete, consistent, enduring, and available. It is a useful check, not a replacement for the regulations and procedures that apply to a record.

AI does not make weak data trustworthy

An AI tool can search, compare, summarize, or flag unusual records. It can also miss a relevant entry, combine two different events, or produce a plausible but incorrect statement. A polished summary is not the original record and should not hide or replace source data.

Keep the source record and its relevant context accessible. Identify which parts were generated or changed with AI, who reviewed them, and what became the official record. Where the system is used to create or change a covered electronic record, assess the applicable controls, including validation, access, audit trail, retrieval, and record copies.

Practical checks for an AI-assisted workflow

  • Attributable: user identity and approval are clear; do not let shared accounts obscure who acted.
  • Contemporaneous: capture records when the activity happens; an AI-generated later narrative must not pretend to be a real-time observation.
  • Original and complete: preserve the source data, metadata, and required audit trail. A summary is not a substitute.
  • Accurate: compare important statements to the source. Check dates, units, names, calculations, and whether the tool left out conflicting evidence.
  • Available: make required records readable and retrievable for the full retention period, even if a vendor service changes.

Do not paste confidential or personal information into a service unless the organization has approved that use and understands how the service handles the information. Define whether prompts and responses are retained, and make sure retention follows the approved recordkeeping process.

Review patterns without hiding individual evidence

AI may help spot repeated deviations or unusual trends across records. Treat those results as leads for investigation. Confirm them against the underlying records, document the review, and consider whether the data set is incomplete or the search terms missed relevant cases.

Key takeaway

Keep the original evidence, history, and responsible person visible. Use AI to help people find and review information, not to blur where a GMP fact came from or who accepted it.
