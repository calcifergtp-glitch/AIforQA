AI in Pharmaceutical Complaint Trending and Signal Detection

Compliance
Published 2026-05-31 • Reviewed 2026-11-26 • 3 min read
CAPA Predictive Analytics Quality Management System Compliance Pharmacovigilance Risk Assessment

AI can help a quality team sort complaint narratives, find possible duplicates, and group similar descriptions. It cannot decide on its own that a complaint is valid, determine the cause, assess patient risk, or decide whether a safety report is required.

In plain words: AI can group reports that sound alike. People check them, investigate the issue, and make the safety and quality decisions.

What complaint procedures require

For drug products subject to 21 CFR Part 211, section 211.198 requires written procedures for handling written and oral complaints and a written record of each complaint. The procedures include review for complaints that may represent a serious and unexpected adverse drug experience requiring reporting. Other reporting duties may apply, including 21 CFR 314.80 for certain postmarketing adverse drug experience reports.

These responsibilities remain with the company. A model’s category or summary is not a complaint investigation, medical assessment, causality decision, or reporting decision.

Where AI may help

  • Suggest possible duplicate records for a reviewer to compare.
  • Group complaint text by product, lot, symptom, or other approved fields.
  • Find similar wording across records, including misspellings and abbreviations.
  • Prepare a source-linked list for trend review.
  • Flag changes in defined counts or categories for human assessment.

AI can miss reports that use different words for the same issue, or group unrelated reports together. A trend signal is a reason to examine evidence, not proof of a common cause or a confirmed safety signal.

Keep sorting separate from decisions

Reviewers should assess the original information and follow the company’s procedures for investigation, product impact, escalation, and reporting. Do not ask a general-purpose model to decide whether a symptom is serious, whether a drug caused it, or whether a reporting deadline applies. Protect personal and health information. Use only systems approved for the information and purpose, and keep the original complaint record intact.

Illustrative example

A reviewer searches for “cracked,” “split,” and “broken tablet.” AI suggests that two records may describe the same issue and mentions one product lot. The reviewer opens the source records, confirms one is a duplicate entry, and routes the other through the existing complaint procedure. The tool helped find and organize text; the reviewer decided how to handle the complaint.

A controlled review process

  1. Define a narrow task, such as finding possible duplicate descriptions.
  2. Use approved data and preserve original records and attachments.
  3. Require source links and make uncertainty visible.
  4. Have trained reviewers confirm groupings, correct errors, and follow established procedures.
  5. Escalate potential safety concerns through required medical, safety, and regulatory channels.
  6. Retain outputs and reviewer decisions when required, and control changes to the tool.

Common mistakes

  • Calling an AI cluster a confirmed safety signal or root cause.
  • Ignoring a complaint because the tool did not flag it.
  • Letting a summary replace the original record.
  • Sending identifiable health information to an unapproved service.

Key takeaway

AI may help teams find and sort complaint records. Keep complaint handling, investigation, patient-safety assessment, and reporting decisions under trained human review and approved procedures.
