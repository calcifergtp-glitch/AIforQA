AI and Change Control Risk Assessment

Compliance
Published 2026-03-14 • Reviewed 2026-11-26 • 3 min read
Change Control Risk Assessment GMP Quality Management System Compliance

AI may help gather information for a change-risk discussion, such as prior deviations, affected procedures, or equipment records. It cannot establish the probability or severity of a risk without suitable evidence, and it should not approve the change.

In plain words: AI can help find possible risks. The team checks which ones are real and decides what controls are needed.

Start with a clear risk question

ICH Q9(R1) says quality risk assessment begins with a clearly defined problem or risk question. It involves identifying hazards, analyzing and evaluating risks, selecting controls, communicating, and reviewing the result. The rigor and formality should fit the uncertainty, importance, and complexity of the issue.

ICH Q10 describes change management that uses quality risk management, appropriate expert review, and post-change evaluation. A risk matrix or numeric score can support a discussion, but it does not replace these steps.

Useful support tasks

  • Search for records connected to the equipment, process, material, or procedure being changed.
  • Draft a list of possible impact areas for subject-matter experts to confirm.
  • Compare proposed controls with approved requirements and identify questions.
  • Summarize assumptions, missing information, and proposed follow-up with source links.

Do not let AI invent likelihood values, severity scores, or rationales. If the source data are sparse or not comparable, make that uncertainty explicit.

Illustrative example

Illustrative only; this is not a real company or reported result. A proposed software update affects a process-monitoring screen. AI finds two prior system changes and suggests that alarm review and operator training may need assessment. IT confirms the technical scope; manufacturing checks affected work instructions; quality evaluates risks and approvals. The team records which impacts apply and why. AI suggested review areas; it did not assign the risk or approve the update.

Safe use checklist

  1. Write the decision question, scope, and assumptions.
  2. Use approved evidence and confirm the correct site, system, product, and version.
  3. Ask AI for source-linked candidates, not a final score.
  4. Have qualified experts assess hazards, likelihood, severity, controls, and uncertainty.
  5. Document decisions, approval, owners, and post-change checks in the change-control system.
  6. Reassess if the proposal or evidence changes.

Key takeaway

AI may organize information for a change-risk discussion. The risk assessment, control choice, and approval remain with the accountable team under its quality system.
