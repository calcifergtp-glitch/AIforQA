Using AI for Regulatory Intelligence

Compliance
2026-05-31 • 3 min read
FDA EMA Compliance GMP Regulatory Intelligence ICH Q10

Regulatory intelligence begins with the real source

Regulatory intelligence means tracking requirements and guidance that may affect a product, process, or quality system. AI can help search official websites, compare document versions, summarize a change, and prepare a list of possible impacts. It can also miss a notice or confuse a draft with a final rule.

Use the official regulator or standards-body page as the source of truth. Record the issuing body, jurisdiction, document type, status, publication or effective date, and the version reviewed. A blog post or AI summary can help you find a source, but it should not replace the source.

A simple review workflow

  1. Collect: monitor the official FDA, EMA, ICH, and other relevant authority pages for your markets.
  2. Classify: separate laws and regulations from final guidance, draft guidance, consultation papers, and news.
  3. Compare: ask AI to identify changes between the official old and new texts, with section links.
  4. Assess: have qualified staff determine which products, procedures, systems, and commitments may be affected.
  5. Act and record: document the decision, owner, due date, and any change-control or training work.

Do not ask the model simply, “What does the new rule require?” Give it the official documents and ask it to quote or point to the supporting sections. Then verify each important statement yourself. If the source is unclear, unavailable, or in a language the reviewers cannot confirm, mark the result as unresolved.

Keep alerts useful and controlled

Set a human owner for each topic and a schedule for checking the official source. AI can reduce repetitive searching, but it should not silently decide that a change is irrelevant or close an action. Keep a record of what was reviewed and why the organization chose to act or not act.

Key takeaway

AI can help you notice and compare documents. People must confirm the official source, understand its legal status, and decide what the change means for the company.
