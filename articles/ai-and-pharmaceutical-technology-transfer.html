AI and Pharmaceutical Technology Transfer

GxP & Validation
Published 2026-05-31 • Reviewed 2026-11-26 • 3 min read
Technology Transfer GMP Validation Knowledge Management Process Validation Risk Assessment

Technology transfer moves product and process knowledge between development, manufacturing, testing, or production sites. AI may help search and compare documents, but it cannot prove that a receiving site is ready or that a process will perform as intended.

In plain words: AI can help find missing information. The sending and receiving teams check the evidence and decide what is ready.

Why knowledge transfer matters

ICH Q10 describes technology transfer as part of the pharmaceutical quality system across the product lifecycle. Knowledge from development, scale-up, and manufacturing can inform the control strategy. FDA’s process-validation guidance also emphasizes process understanding and lifecycle evidence. The required activities depend on the product, process, change, and applicable rules.

Where AI may help

  • Search approved process descriptions, specifications, methods, and change records.
  • Compare document versions and flag differences for expert review.
  • Build a draft list of open questions, dependencies, and missing records.
  • Summarize scale-up or site history with citations to source documents.
  • Help track transfer actions and owners after the team approves them.

A difference between documents is not automatically an error. A missing file in a search result does not prove that the information does not exist. Review the controlled source and the transfer plan.

Keep technical decisions with the transfer team

Do not use AI alone to select process parameters, approve a method, determine comparability, authorize production, or conclude that a site is qualified. Those conclusions need appropriate scientific evidence, risk assessment, documented responsibilities, and approved quality decisions. Protect confidential product and process information; only use AI services approved for the data.

Illustrative example

Illustrative only; this is not a real company or reported result. A receiving site asks an approved tool to compare two versions of a packaging instruction. The tool highlights a changed line-clearance step and links to both versions. The sending and receiving subject-matter experts check the approved documents, confirm whether the change was intentional, and update the transfer action list. AI found a difference; the experts determined its meaning.

A controlled transfer workflow

  1. Define the product, process, sites, and records within scope.
  2. Use current controlled documents and verify version, units, and identifiers.
  3. Ask AI to locate gaps and differences with source links.
  4. Have the responsible experts review technical impact and update the transfer plan.
  5. Document responsibilities, approvals, qualification evidence, and remaining risks.
  6. Route changes through established change control and maintain the record set.

Key takeaway

AI may make transfer documents easier to search and compare. It cannot replace process understanding, receiving-site qualification, or quality approval. Use it to find questions, then resolve them through the approved transfer process.
