AI in Pharmaceutical Maintenance and Calibration Programs

GxP & Validation
Published 2026-05-31 • Reviewed 2026-11-26 • 3 min read
GMP Validation Predictive Analytics Equipment Qualification GxP Calibration

AI can help maintenance and metrology teams find patterns in work orders, alarms, and calibration records. That can help a person decide what to inspect next. A model prediction is not proof that equipment will fail, and it does not change an approved maintenance or calibration program.

In plain words: AI can point to a machine that may need a closer look. A qualified person checks the records and decides what work is needed.

What the GMP rules require

For drug products covered by 21 CFR Part 211, section 211.67 requires equipment cleaning and maintenance procedures, appropriate intervals, and records. For laboratory instruments and recording devices, section 211.160(b)(4) requires calibration at suitable intervals under a written program that defines directions, schedules, accuracy limits, and remedial actions when limits are not met. These requirements apply to the relevant activities and equipment.

The regulations do not require AI-based failure predictions. The company must follow its procedures and assess the effect of equipment problems on product quality and records.

Useful support tasks

  • Search work orders and group similar symptoms for a person to compare.
  • Flag records that appear overdue or incomplete for checking against the approved schedule.
  • Prepare a source-linked equipment history before a maintenance review.
  • Highlight changes in defined measures, such as alarm counts, for investigation.

These tasks organize evidence. They do not prove a root cause, establish a reliable failure probability, or approve an equipment state.

Maintenance and calibration are different

Maintenance addresses equipment condition and function. Calibration checks whether a measuring device performs within defined accuracy limits. A model that sees fewer breakdowns does not show that a calibration interval can be extended. Any schedule change must follow the organization’s approved process and be supported by appropriate technical evidence and quality review. A model should not silently change due dates, place equipment in service, or close a required record.

Illustrative example

Illustrative only; this is not a real company or reported result. A planner asks an AI tool to group work orders for one filling line. It links several “intermittent sensor” notes and points to recent alarms. An engineer checks the original records and finds that one note belongs to a different sensor. The engineer inspects the correct device and records the outcome under the existing procedure. The tool helped locate records; the engineer verified the equipment and chose what to do.

A controlled way to use AI

  1. Pick one narrow task, such as finding repeated work-order descriptions for an asset.
  2. Use approved records and confirm asset and instrument identifiers.
  3. Require source links for material statements and let reviewers open the records.
  4. Have the responsible engineer or metrologist verify alerts and document decisions.
  5. Assess access, changes, data connections, and applicable system controls for the intended use.

Key takeaway

AI may make equipment records easier to search and compare. Keep the approved program in control: verify alerts against original records, document decisions, and do not treat a prediction as permission to change an interval or equipment status.
