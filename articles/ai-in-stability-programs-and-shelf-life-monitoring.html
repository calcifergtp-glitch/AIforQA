Artificial Intelligence in Stability Programs and Shelf-Life Monitoring

GxP & Validation
Published 2026-05-29 • Reviewed 2026-11-26 • 3 min read
Stability Programs GMP Predictive Analytics ICH Guidelines Validation Data Integrity

AI can help stability teams organize study data, spot missing results, and flag patterns for a closer look. A model does not establish shelf life, change storage instructions, or decide how an excursion affects a product.

In plain words: AI can point out a pattern in stability data. Qualified experts check the data and make the scientific and regulatory decision.

What stability evidence is for

Stability studies provide evidence about how a drug substance or product changes over time under specified conditions. ICH Q1A(R2) describes stability-study expectations. ICH Q1E provides approaches for evaluating stability data, including statistical methods and conditions for extrapolation. Requirements depend on the product and applicable regulatory framework.

Results must be interpreted with knowledge of the product, methods, batches, packaging, storage conditions, and study design. A model output cannot replace the approved protocol, analytical procedures, investigations, or required quality and regulatory review.

AI tasks that may support the team

  • Check whether scheduled results or study records appear to be missing.
  • Organize results by product, batch, package, time point, and storage condition.
  • Chart trends for a scientist to assess against the protocol and criteria.
  • Flag data points for investigation without deleting or dismissing them.
  • Prepare a source-linked overview of open investigations or study milestones.

These tasks may help with navigation and review preparation. They do not establish a degradation mechanism, prove an out-of-trend result, or support a shelf-life change by themselves.

Why predictions need caution

Datasets may be small, contain few time points, or combine different formulations, packages, methods, or batches. A model from one product may not transfer to another. Missing results, protocol deviations, method changes, or unrepresentative batches can distort a trend. A forecast can hide those limits unless its assumptions and source data are visible.

Do not use AI output alone to extend or shorten shelf life, change a retest period or storage statement, or disposition product after an excursion. Assess any proposed change through the established scientific, quality, and regulatory process using suitable data.

Illustrative example

Illustrative only; this is not a real company or reported result. A stability analyst asks a tool to chart assay results for one study. It highlights a point that appears higher than the prior results. The analyst checks the raw data, method version, calculations, sample handling, and protocol, then follows the laboratory procedure to decide whether an investigation is needed. The chart drew attention to a point; the analyst assessed its meaning.

A practical review workflow

  1. Confirm product, batch, package, storage condition, study, method, and time point.
  2. Check data completeness and correct record links.
  3. Review alerts against the protocol, criteria, method, and product knowledge.
  4. Investigate unusual results; do not silently remove inconvenient points.
  5. Document assumptions, tool version, reviewer corrections, and the assessment.
  6. Route shelf-life, storage, or regulatory changes through required approvals.

Key takeaway

AI may help stability teams see and organize data. Stability conclusions still require scientific evaluation of the study, product, methods, and applicable requirements. Keep the model in a support role and route shelf-life decisions through qualified review.
