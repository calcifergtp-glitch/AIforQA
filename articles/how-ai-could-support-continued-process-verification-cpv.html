How AI Could Support Continued Process Verification (CPV)

GxP & Validation
Published 2026-05-31 • Reviewed 2026-11-26 • 3 min read
Continued Process Verification CPV Validation GMP Predictive Analytics Process Validation

Continued process verification (CPV) means collecting and reviewing process and product information during routine production to check whether a process remains in a state of control. AI may help organize data and flag changes. A model output does not prove that a process is controlled or out of control.

In plain words: AI can point to a trend worth checking. Trained people decide what the trend means and what action is needed.

What CPV is for

FDA’s process-validation guidance describes Stage 3 as ongoing assurance that the process remains in a state of control. It calls for collecting and analyzing relevant process and product data, statistically trending and reviewing information, and using trained personnel. ICH Q10 describes process-performance and product-quality monitoring across the product lifecycle.

AI is one possible analysis aid. It does not replace process knowledge, the approved control strategy, investigations, or required quality review.

Possible AI support tasks

  • Combine approved trends from process parameters, incoming materials, in-process tests, and finished-product results.
  • Flag unusual values or changes in established indicators for review.
  • Help find related deviations, changes, and maintenance records.
  • Draft a source-linked summary for a periodic process review.
  • Check whether required data appear missing before the review.

These are signals for examination. A change in one chart can have several explanations, including normal variation, a changed measurement method, a data problem, or a true process shift.

Build the review around data and process knowledge

Before using a model, define which products, processes, batches, measures, and time windows it covers. Confirm that identifiers, units, timestamps, and methods are consistent. Decide what counts as a flag and how a reviewer will check it. Show missing data and uncertainty instead of filling gaps with guesses.

Do not let an AI alert automatically change process limits, update a control strategy, initiate a batch disposition, or close a deviation. Use the approved change-control and investigation pathways.

Illustrative example

Illustrative only; this is not a real company or reported result. A CPV review tool highlights a shift in mixing time across recent batches. The process engineer checks the underlying records and finds that the units changed after an equipment software update. The team corrects its data mapping and reviews the trend again before drawing a conclusion. The alert helped identify a question; it did not establish process drift.

A practical review cycle

  1. Set the scope and define meaningful measures with process and quality experts.
  2. Check source data, units, missing values, methods, and changes in equipment or procedures.
  3. Review each model flag against source records and process knowledge.
  4. Document whether the trend is expected, needs more study, or requires investigation.
  5. Approve any resulting action through existing quality and change-control procedures.
  6. Monitor the tool itself for missed flags, unnecessary alerts, and changes that affect intended use.

Key takeaway

AI may help teams see and organize CPV trends. A trend flag is not a quality conclusion. Maintain data integrity, use trained review, and keep the process owner and Quality Unit responsible for interpreting results and approving action.
