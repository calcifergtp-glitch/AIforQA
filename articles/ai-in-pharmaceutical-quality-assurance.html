AI in Pharmaceutical Quality Assurance: Opportunities, Risks, and Real Tools

GxP & Validation
2026-03-10 • 3 min read
Quality Management System GxP AI Governance Compliance Risk Assessment FDA

Quality assurance supports safe, reliable decisions

Pharmaceutical quality assurance is the work of making sure processes, records, and products meet the requirements that apply to them. It includes review, oversight, investigation, change control, training, supplier controls, and improvement. In U.S. drug CGMP, the quality control unit has specific responsibilities and authority under 21 CFR 211.22.

AI may help search records, compare documents, summarize trends, draft text for review, or prioritize items for a person to examine. These uses can save time, but they do not transfer the organization’s responsibilities to the software vendor or model.

Choose a narrow job for AI

Start with one task and one clear boundary. For example: “find prior deviations that mention this equipment and show the source records.” Define what data it can access, what its output means, and who checks it. Keep the tool from approving a batch, closing a CAPA, or changing a controlled record unless the specific process and system are appropriately designed, assessed, and authorized.

Use checks that match the risk

  • Compare summaries to original records and check that important exceptions were not lost.
  • Require a qualified person to approve consequential quality decisions.
  • Protect confidential information and limit access to approved users.
  • Keep traceable records of system use and final decisions where required by the process.
  • Review performance and user-reported mistakes; pause use if safeguards fail.

AI may also help identify repeated issues across investigations. Treat a pattern as a lead, not proof of a root cause. Investigators need to examine the underlying evidence, consider alternative explanations, and document why the conclusion is supported.

Be clear about what is not established

A vendor’s demonstration does not prove that a tool is fit for a particular GMP workflow. A high accuracy score from a small test set does not guarantee reliable performance on all products or records. Assess the intended use, risks, configuration, data, human review, and changes over time.

Key takeaway

AI can help QA staff find and organize information. People and the quality system remain responsible for evaluating evidence, approving actions, and protecting patients and product quality.
