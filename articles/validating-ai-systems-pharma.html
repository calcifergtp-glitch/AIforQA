Validating AI Systems in Pharma

GxP & Validation
2026-03-14 • 4 min read
Validation AI Governance GxP Computer System Validation Risk Assessment Machine Learning

Start with the job, not the model

Before using AI in a pharmaceutical process, define its intended job in plain terms. Say what input it may use, what output it may produce, who checks that output, and which decisions stay with qualified people. A tool that drafts a summary is different from a tool that recommends a product-quality decision.

FDA and the European Medicines Agency published guiding principles for good AI practice in drug development. Those principles support careful development, reliable evidence, transparency, and monitoring. Their stated focus is drug development; they do not replace the rules for manufacturing, laboratories, or quality systems. For GxP operations, apply the existing requirements that fit the actual workflow.

Build evidence for the intended use

  • Check the data: confirm the source is approved, relevant, current, and suitable for the task. Watch for missing records, inconsistent terms, and sensitive information.
  • Try realistic cases: test common work, edge cases, unclear inputs, and examples where the right response is to stop or ask a person.
  • Measure errors that matter: look for missed issues, invented facts, wrong classifications, and differences across user groups or operating conditions.
  • Make review practical: show the evidence behind a result and give the reviewer a clear way to correct or reject it.
  • Record the setup: identify the model or service version, configuration, data sources, and approval used for the evaluation.

There is no single accuracy number that proves an AI feature is safe for every task. Decide what acceptable performance means for this specific use, how errors could affect patients or product quality, and what safeguards reduce those risks.

Control changes and watch performance

Some AI services change over time. Treat a new model version, major prompt change, new data source, or changed workflow as a possible system change. Assess its effect before use and perform checks proportional to the risk. If the supplier cannot identify what changed, limit the feature to low-risk work or pause it until the impact can be understood.

After release, review problems and trends. Set a person who can restrict or turn off the feature. Reassess it when the process, data, or intended use changes. For important decisions, preserve the human review and approval step in the controlled procedure.

Key takeaway

AI validation is not a one-time model score. It is evidence that a defined, limited workflow works as intended, plus controls for people, records, changes, and ongoing performance.
