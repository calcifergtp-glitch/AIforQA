AI and Audit Trail Review: The Next Evolution of Data Integrity

Compliance
Published 2026-05-29 • Reviewed 2026-11-26 • 3 min read
Audit Trail Review Data Integrity 21 CFR Part 11 ALCOA+ Compliance Annex 11

An audit trail records information about actions or changes in a computerized system. AI may help search events and highlight patterns for review. A flagged event does not prove wrongdoing, and the absence of a flag does not prove that a record is reliable.

In plain words: AI can point to a change worth checking. A reviewer opens the original record and decides whether the change matters.

Start with the applicable records and rules

For drug manufacturing, applicable CGMP rules require records and systems to be controlled and reliable. FDA’s Part 11 scope-and-application guidance explains how Part 11 applies alongside predicate-rule requirements and recommends a documented, risk-based approach to audit trails or other controls. The details depend on the record, system, intended use, and applicable requirements.

AI does not make an audit trail complete or compliant. Preserve the original electronic record, relevant metadata, and the system’s controls. Do not use an AI summary as a substitute for required records or review procedures.

Tasks AI may support

  • Search event records by user, time, record, action, or reason.
  • Group repeated changes or late entries for a reviewer to examine.
  • Compare a change with the associated approved procedure or record.
  • Prepare a source-linked event summary and list of unresolved questions.
  • Flag unusual patterns using criteria defined and reviewed by the organization.

A pattern may have an ordinary explanation, such as a procedure, system configuration, or timing issue. The reviewer needs context and the original source.

Human review and documentation

Define which events should be reviewed, who reviews them, how often, and how concerns are escalated. A qualified reviewer should verify the event, affected record, user role, timestamp, reason, and impact. Document the assessment and any follow-up under the applicable procedure. Do not let AI assign intent, declare fraud, invalidate data, or close an investigation.

Illustrative example

Illustrative only; this is not a real company or reported result. A reviewer asks a tool to find repeated edits to one result field. The tool links several events to the source records. The reviewer checks the sequence and sees that some edits were permitted corrections while one needs a documented explanation. The reviewer follows the approved review procedure. AI grouped events; the reviewer assessed them.

Controls to consider

  • Use approved, complete event data and verify system, user, time, and record identifiers.
  • Keep source links, original values, changed values, and context visible.
  • Test for missed events and false alarms using representative records.
  • Control access, configuration changes, model versions, and records of human decisions.
  • Assess privacy and confidentiality before using third-party AI services.

Key takeaway

AI may help reviewers find events in large records. It cannot establish intent or replace record controls and human review. Keep the original evidence intact and document decisions through the approved quality system.
