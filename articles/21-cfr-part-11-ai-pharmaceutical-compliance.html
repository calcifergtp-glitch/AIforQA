21 CFR Part 11 and AI in Pharmaceutical Systems

Compliance
2026-03-07 • 4 min read • Reviewed November 26, 2026
21 CFR Part 11 Data Integrity FDA Electronic Records Compliance Validation

First ask whether the record is covered

21 CFR Part 11 covers certain electronic records and electronic signatures. It works together with the FDA rule that says the record must exist, such as a drug manufacturing record. That underlying rule is often called the predicate rule. Part 11 does not apply to every file just because it is electronic or uses AI.

FDA’s scope guidance explains that Part 11 generally applies when required records are created, changed, kept, retrieved, or sent electronically, and when electronic signatures replace handwritten signatures. A company should map each record and workflow to the applicable rule and its own recordkeeping process.

What changes when AI is involved?

An AI tool may summarize a deviation, draft a procedure, or flag a trend. The company still needs to decide what counts as the official record, where it is stored, who reviews it, and how the final decision is approved. If the AI output becomes part of a covered record, the controlled system and workflow need controls suitable for that use.

Part 11 does not certify an AI model or make its answer correct. A model’s confident wording is not evidence that the facts are right. Keep the source information available, require a qualified person to check important claims, and record who approved the final result.

Controls teams should define

  • Access: only authorized people can use the system or approve a record.
  • Review and signatures: signatures identify the person, date, and meaning of the approval, and stay linked to the record.
  • Audit trail: important changes are recorded with who changed what and when; earlier information is not silently overwritten.
  • Copies and retention: accurate records can be retrieved and read for the required retention period.
  • Validation: evidence shows the configured system and workflow work for their intended use.

These controls come from the applicable regulation and the firm’s approved procedures. The exact design depends on the system, its use, and risk. A vendor’s statement that a product is “Part 11 compliant” does not replace the company’s own assessment.

A practical example

If an AI assistant drafts a batch-record investigation, keep the approved source data and the human-reviewed final record in the controlled system. Make clear who checked and approved the conclusion. Do not let an unreviewed model response automatically release a batch, close a deviation, or replace a required record.

Key takeaway

Start with the record and the rule that requires it. Then check whether it is electronic, how AI touches it, and which controls are needed to protect its accuracy, history, approval, and retrieval.
