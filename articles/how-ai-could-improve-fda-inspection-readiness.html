How AI Could Improve FDA Inspection Readiness

Compliance
2026-05-01 • 3 min read
Inspection Readiness FDA Audit Trail Review Data Integrity Compliance 21 CFR Part 11

Readiness improves when gaps are found early

AI may help quality teams search records, compare required fields, spot overdue actions, or identify repeated themes across investigations. These signals can help staff decide where to look more closely. They do not predict what an investigator will find or prove that a process is compliant.

Use a defined data set and explain what the tool checked. A dashboard that misses records, uses outdated procedures, or counts every flagged item as a failure can mislead the team. Keep the underlying records available so staff can check each finding.

Useful, limited tasks

  • Find required records that appear missing from a defined inspection package.
  • Flag records with blank fields, mismatched identifiers, or incomplete approval steps.
  • Group related deviations or CAPAs so an investigator can review the actual cases.
  • Check whether requested documents can be retrieved in a readable format.
  • Create a mock question list from approved procedures for staff practice.

For every flagged gap, a person should confirm the correct requirement, inspect the official record, and assign follow-up through the established quality process. A missing field may be a real defect, a data-mapping problem, or a false alert. Record how it was resolved.

Do not confuse a score with readiness

AI-generated risk scores are only as sound as their inputs and assumptions. A low score cannot guarantee an inspection will go smoothly, and a high score does not by itself establish a compliance failure. Avoid using an unvalidated model to rate staff, hide problems, or decide which records to withhold.

Before relying on an AI feature, define the intended use, source data, human checks, and how changes are controlled. Protect confidential information and ensure the process can retrieve the required underlying records independently of the AI service.

Key takeaway

AI can make routine checks faster and surface questions for follow-up. Inspection readiness comes from fixing real issues, maintaining accessible records, and being able to explain decisions with evidence.
