Computer System Validation for AI Software

GxP & Validation
2026-03-14 • 4 min read • Reviewed November 26, 2026
Computer System Validation CSV GxP Validation Risk Assessment 21 CFR Part 11

Validation answers one practical question

Computer system validation is documented evidence that a configured system works reliably for the job the company uses it to do. The goal is not to test every button. It is to show that important workflows give dependable results, protect records, and have controls that catch problems.

For drug CGMP, FDA points to the intended use of the computer system and the risk to product quality and patient safety. 21 CFR 211.68 also requires appropriate controls over computerized equipment and related systems. The company remains responsible even when a vendor supplies the software.

Define the intended use before testing

Write down what the system may do, what it must not do, who uses it, and which decisions depend on it. For an AI feature, be specific: does it search approved procedures, draft text for review, classify incoming records, or calculate a value? “Uses AI for quality” is too vague to validate.

Draw the workflow boundary. Include data sources, model or service, configuration, connected systems, user review, final record, and any export or interface. Identify which output is advisory and which action creates or changes a controlled record.

Test the risks that matter

  • Use representative examples, including unusual but realistic inputs.
  • Check that source data, units, dates, and context are carried through correctly.
  • Try incomplete, conflicting, or misleading inputs and confirm the workflow stops or asks for review.
  • Confirm user permissions, approvals, audit trails, record copies, backup, and retrieval work as designed.
  • Check the handoff to connected systems and make sure an AI suggestion cannot silently become an approved decision.

Document the test, expected result, actual result, reviewer, and resolution of failures. Match the depth of testing to the risk. A tool that only searches public material has a different risk from one that writes into a batch record or changes a quality status.

Keep it in a controlled state

Set rules for changes to the model, prompts, configuration, source documents, and vendor service. Decide what changes require impact assessment or additional testing. Monitor performance and user-reported errors, and define when to suspend the feature. Keep the version and configuration used for important work identifiable.

Vendor testing can help, but it does not prove the company’s own configured workflow is suitable. The company should retain enough evidence to explain how the system was assessed and why it is fit for its intended use.

Key takeaway

Validate the whole job the AI feature performs in your process: inputs, output, human check, recordkeeping, and system connections. Keep the scope focused on intended use and risk.
